# Home page research showcase

## Goals

- Put the CV, email, ResearchGate, and every other configured social link below the introductory text. Show the contact note there too, with no duplicate icon row at the bottom.
- Place five research highlights after the bio and before News. The newest highlight is a full-width, frameless video at the top. Each of the other four is a row with a plain-language paragraph beside a frameless, transparent video, and the video alternates sides from row to row: border-cell migration (video right), Navier–Stokes (left), observability (right), piezoelectric beams (left). Phones use one column throughout.
- Give each highlight a research image or animation and a working project link.
- Match each card title to its Research page title and keep the cards free of separate description text.

The cards run from the newest work to the oldest, in the same order as the Research page, which sorts by each project's `importance` (1 comes first).

The cell migration page is a plain-language overview of the 3D phase-field model in the reconstructed, growing egg chamber (run `exp_gd_G2_k0`), with no equations. Its media live in `assets/img/projects/border-cell-migration/` and were made from that run's `migration.avi` and the `videos_growth_density/` folder of the PFM_Naghmeh project. Labels for growth factor, κ, and model time were removed and replaced with plain titles, and the 3D animation was cropped to the chamber with its JPEG background cleaned to white. The slice figure `push-arrows-slice.png` was redrawn in Python from the `slices.mat` that the project's `tools/tim_slice_figures/make_tim_slice_figures.m` writes for this run at 50% progress. Its arrows are the in-plane part of the TIM vector on the plane along the cluster path. The research index uses `thumbnail-growing-3x2.png`, the 3D still padded with white to 3:2. The home page uses `cell-migration-growth.mp4`, a see-through re-render of the same run in the style of the cluster video (nurse cells see-through gray, oocyte see-through purple, cluster blue, transparent background) from `tools/website_assets/render_home_growth.m` in the PFM_Naghmeh project. It runs from the start to arrival (t = 592) with the camera sized for the final chamber, so the growth shows. Snapshots are 20 time units apart; the cluster is shifted along its recorded path before two snapshots are blended, so it does not appear doubled. It is encoded as a stacked-alpha MP4 like the cluster video and played by the same script. The paragraph beside it describes both models without equations: the guidance signal spreading through the spaces between the cells, the coupling between cell shapes and that signal, the chamber growth (stage 9), and the hybrid optimal-transport cluster.

The Navier–Stokes card is frameless and plays `navier-stokes-flow-pressure.mp4`, a stacked-alpha video in the same format as the cell videos, with a transparent background and nothing drawn but the channel. It stacks three copies of the channel: the magnitude of the mean velocity with tracer particles carried by the mean velocity (top), the mean pressure with isobars (middle), and the RMS standard deviation of the velocity, sqrt(Var(u_x) + Var(u_y)) (bottom). Speed and standard deviation use fixed color limits over the run, so the start-up shows. The pressure is scaled to its own range at each moment, because the pressure drop during start-up is about 40 times its final value; its colors show where the pressure is high and low, not its size. The flow develops over the first five seconds (the saved solution from t = 0.001 to 1.027, sped up at the start where it changes fastest), then holds the last state while the tracers keep moving, and dissolves back to the start. Tracer speed is scaled for viewing and is not physical time. The fields come from the saved TT-SGFEM solution `manuscript_nc4_b8_Re100_lognormal_KL2PK6P3_CoV10_results.mat` via `visual/export_home_flow.m` in the stochNS project (no solver runs), which writes `results/videos/home_flow/flow_fields.mat`. Each channel has a short HTML title above it (Flow speed, Pressure, Uncertainty), placed in the gaps the render leaves for them; if the channel positions in the render change, update the `.home-flow-label` offsets. A Python script draws the 1152 × 1072 frames from that file with their opacity, so no black/white matting is needed; it is kept with the other render scripts outside this repository. The paragraph beside the video explains the uncertain viscosity, the stochastic Galerkin solver (all time steps together, compressed low-rank storage) and what each channel shows, without equations. The observability and piezoelectric videos were made without the original source code, from the project pages' videos (`unfiltered-discretization.mp4` and `orfd-discretization.mp4`, 806 × 450; `piezoelectric-beam-stabilization.mp4`, 1528 × 1062; all 10 frames per second). The in-video titles ("beam at time", "total energy", "sensor", "actuator") are cut out and shown as larger HTML titles in the gaps the render leaves (`.home-obs-label--*` for Unfiltered and ORFD, and `.home-dial-label` spans with inline positions for the dials); the dials keep their tick numbers and units. For observability, the static gray 3D box lines are removed (pixels that match the per-pixel median over time and are neutral gray), the beam band is kept at full size with a soft top and bottom edge (the beam leaves it in about 2% of frames), the dials are kept at full size, and the Unfiltered video is stacked above the ORFD one (806 × 868). The piezoelectric video keeps the beam, clamp, wires and voltage dial above the three dials, scaled to 1146 × 622. White becomes transparent: neutral ink (black lines, text, gray arcs) becomes black with opacity equal to its darkness, saturated colors (beams, needles, energy bars) stay opaque, and the piezoelectric beam and clamp are kept opaque. Black ink would vanish on the dark theme, so the player script turns black and dark-gray ink light in dark mode inside the rectangles given by each video's `data-ink-rects` attribute (the dial rows, and the voltage dial with its wires); the beams keep their own colors. The transparent stills have dark-theme copies (`*-poster-dark.webp`) with the same ink change, swapped in by CSS. Each loop's last second dissolves into its first. The scripts that build these videos are kept with the other render scripts outside this repository. The paragraphs beside them explain, without equations, why standard discretizations hide vibrations from the tip sensor and how ORFD fixes it, and how the two feedback gains of the piezoelectric beam are chosen.

The supplied publishing notes asked for a final check of the three-dimensional manuscript's “in preparation” status. The arXiv and PLOS reference details were checked against their public records.

At desktop widths of 840 px and above, the profile photo is 184 px wide so its bottom lines up with the contact note. Smaller layouts keep the theme's original photo sizing.

## Updating the media

1. To change a thumbnail, update that card's `img src` in `_pages/about.md`, or replace its current asset while keeping the same filename. Video can be converted to an animated WebP for an image-based card. If embedding a video directly, add it under `assets/video/`, replace the card's `<img>` with a `<video>` element, and provide a poster image and a useful caption or transcript.
2. Keep each card title aligned with its project title on the Research page. Update image alternative text when the media changes. If a video's meaning depends on motion or narration, add a nearby caption or transcript.
3. Run `npm run lint:prettier`, `npm run lint:style-contract`, and `bundle exec jekyll build --baseurl /al-folio`. Check the home page in desktop and mobile views, including all five links and the News section below the showcase.

The cell-cluster card (`.home-research-card--featured`) spans both grid columns and has no border or background, so its video sits directly on the page. The video `cell-cluster-zoom.mp4` shows run `ot_eq8_base` in the 3D chamber from t = 498 to 723, then holds that snapshot while the camera zooms in until the cluster fills the frame and the chamber fades out. The border cells turn see-through to show the polar cells for a few seconds, turn solid again, and the camera zooms back out while the migration resumes and runs to arrival (t = 902). The camera swings around the cluster while zoomed in and turns back while zooming out, so the whole chamber stays oblique enough to fit the frame. A small HTML legend (border cells, polar cells) beside the cluster is timed to the close-up by the `data-from`/`data-to` video times on its items; if the render's segment lengths in `home_zoom` change, update those times. Its frames come from `render_web_media('home')` in the research folder's `webpage_highlights/scripts/`, which writes every frame on a black and on a white background to `webpage_highlights/home_zoom_frames/`. The opacity is one minus the difference of the two renders, and the black render is the premultiplied color. The MP4 stores each 1280 × 720 frame as a 1280 × 1440 "stacked alpha" image (color on top, opacity below), and the inline script at the end of `_pages/about.md` recombines the halves with WebGL on a canvas, so the background is transparent in every browser, Safari included. Both videos loop with a dissolve: the last 24 frames blend into the first 24 (premultiplied color and opacity mixed linearly), and the encoded video starts at source frame 25, so the page never goes empty at the restart. The legend times are therefore one second earlier than the render's own frame times. `cell-cluster-zoom-poster.webp` is a transparent still of the video's first frame; it shows when WebGL is unavailable or the visitor prefers reduced motion. The project page `_projects/cell-cluster-migration.md` is a plain-language overview of the ten-cell optimal-transport cluster model. Its media come from the 13 November 2026 version of that folder, whose main run is `ot_eq8_base`. The GIFs were cropped below the title row (which carries the time), subsampled, and given a 128-color dithered palette for 3D renders and a 96-color undithered palette for flat sections. The page shows only the four-version comparison (`05_four_variants_side_by_side.mp4`), not the single-ingredient videos, and has no work-in-progress section. `chamber-overview-v2.png`, `four-moments-v2.png` and `guidance-signal-v2.png` are resized copies of `new01`, `new02` and `new04` in `figures/`. The `-v2` suffix on the regenerated media keeps browsers from showing cached copies of the earlier versions. The research-index `thumbnail-3x2.png` is a frame of `01_migration_3d.mp4`, padded to 3:2 so it matches the other Research cards (all between about 1.4 and 1.6). `cell-polarity-slices.png` was drawn with the research folder's `webpage_highlights/scripts/render_web_media.m` section style, using an added `arrows` job, on run `ot_eq8_base` at 60% and 82% of its arrival time. Its arrows are the in-plane part of each border cell's polarity `p`, drawn only for cells whose centroid lies within 0.16 of the cut. The production build converts each GIF into animated WebP at 480, 800, and 1400 px, so the GIFs are only the fallback download.

## Research pages

Figures and most videos on the Research project pages stay on their white background, framed as before. Only pure 3D renders get a transparent background, and only when they can be rendered again from the simulation data, the way the homepage videos are: each frame is drawn once on black and once on white, and the difference gives the exact opacity. Cutting the white out of finished images afterwards was tried and dropped, because filled plot panels, soft halos and compression noise came out as patchy cut-outs.

- The cell migration page opens with the homepage's growing-chamber video (`research-highlights/cell-migration-growth.mp4`, from `render_home_growth.m` in the PFM_Naghmeh project).
- The cell-cluster page's close-up (`cluster-closeup-alpha.mp4`) comes from `render_web_media('closeup_alpha')` in the PFM_Naghmeh_transport project's `webpage_highlights/scripts/`. It shows run `ot_eq8_base` from the start to arrival, every 4th cell snapshot, with the outside view and the see-through view side by side. It has no titles, legend or clock in the render; the page shows the view titles (`.tmedia-label`) and the color key (`.tmedia-legend`) as HTML, so they read in both themes.
- Markup: a `span.tmedia` with the transparent poster, a canvas and the video. `assets/js/transparent-media.js` recombines the color (top half) and the opacity (bottom half) with WebGL and plays the video only while it is on screen; `assets/css/transparent-media.css` holds the styles. Each page that uses it links the stylesheet at its top and the script at its end.
- `archive/` (local, not in git, excluded from the build) keeps media that no page uses any more, the frames-to-video script `scripts/assemble_stacked_alpha.py`, and the scripts of the dropped cut-out attempt.

The Navier–Stokes page explains the model in words and keeps only the governing equations, short enough to fit a phone screen.

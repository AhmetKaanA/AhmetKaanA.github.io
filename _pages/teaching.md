I enjoy helping students develop both mathematical technique and the confidence to explain why a method works. My teaching emphasizes careful interpretation of notation, connections among representations, regular low-stakes practice, and opportunities for students to revisit ideas as their understanding develops.

## Teaching development and recognition

I received UMBC's **Outstanding Graduate Teaching Assistant in the Field of Mathematics** recognition in 2026. My formal preparation also includes a one-year **Pedagogical Formation** program at Istanbul University (2018–2019), which included supervised teaching of ninth- and tenth-grade mathematics, and Graduate Teaching Assistant Institute training at Western Kentucky University.

I completed teaching certifications at the **Scholar** and **Practitioner** levels through the [Center for the Integration of Research, Teaching, and Learning at UMBC](https://gspd.umbc.edu/about-cirtl/). CIRTL supports the development of evidence-based, inclusive, and reflective teaching practices for future faculty. Additional information is available through the [CIRTL Network](https://cirtl.net/).

My work toward these certifications included the following teaching-development activities:

- Eighth Annual Provost’s Teaching & Learning Symposium
- Being an Effective Teaching Assistant
- How to Design Your Course
- Master Yoda Training: Mentorship Skills Every Grad Student Needs
- Effective Instructional Strategies
- Classroom Management Strategies 101
- Digging into Student Learning Data: Creating Measured Curricular Change
- In Front of a Class: Public Speaking Tips
- Better Teaching Through Neurodiversity: Supporting Students’ Executive Functioning
- Leveraging Strengths of Neurodiverse Students in the Classroom

I also completed a CIRTL Teaching-as-Research project, [Developing Students' Symbol Sense with Targeted Practice]({% link _projects/symbol-sense-targeted-practice.md %}), which examines how targeted examples can help students interpret mathematical notation and reason through ambiguity.

## Scholarship of Teaching and Learning

My current SoTL project, [Developing Students' Symbol Sense with Targeted Practice]({% link _projects/symbol-sense-targeted-practice.md %}), investigates how brief, low-stakes exercises can help students move beyond procedural symbol manipulation and interpret mathematical expressions structurally. The project uses targeted practice, written explanation, and paired confidence-and-accuracy measures to study changes in students' notational awareness.

## Orientation Advising

Since Summer 2024, I have served as an [Orientation Advisor at UMBC](https://advising.umbc.edu/orientation/). Through this work, I have individually advised more than 300 incoming students from a wide range of majors, academic backgrounds, and educational experiences. During each appointment, I help students review their prior coursework, discuss their intended major and academic interests, identify appropriate courses, and develop a schedule that supports progress toward graduation. Orientation advising complements my classroom teaching by giving me a broader view of individual needs and ambitions of students.
